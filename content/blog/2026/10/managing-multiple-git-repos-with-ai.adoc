No single person can know everything happening in Fedora at any given time.
I say this often in our community, half as a warning and half for comfort.
For a while, I did not really believe it applied to me.
I wanted to know everything.
I joined new teams, participated in tickets, and either participated or led meetings across the project.
Surprisingly, as a link:/tags/rochester-institute-of-technology/[young university student], I held a vast amount of it in my head.

But Fedora does not stand still long enough for that to last.
Eventually, I was spread thin across more teams than I could realistically track.
As my academic load increased or I began an internship, promises I made quietly fell off my radar.
It was not because I did not care.
It was because the systems I used to track them could not keep up with the sprawl, when my available time to contribute changed.

However, as I reentered the Fedora community as a link:/categories/red-hat/[Red Hat employee], the same systems I used as a student were challenged.
As I get going with link:/blog/2026/09/ai-alignment/[my new role], I am rethinking how I can rebalance my bandwidth sustainably.
So, this post is about how I started managing multiple Git repos with AI agents to claw back some of that bandwidth.
It is not really a story about a remarkable tool.
It is an argument that in a project this sprawling, protecting your attention is the real win.
I will cover two moments when things fell through the cracks, the workflow I built to catch them, a real example of it working, and an honest take on trusting AI when it sometimes gets things wrong.


[[scale]]
== Why managing multiple Git repos gets hard at Fedora's scale

Fedora is sprawling.
Fedora's link:https://src.fedoraproject.org[distribution Git (dist-git)], the home for package sources, holds something like 20,000 packages.
Add the team and SIG repositories that coordinate the people behind those packages on link:https://forge.fedoraproject.org[Fedora Forge], and the number of places where decisions get made is enormous.
Yet, most contributors only touch a handful of them.
However, even a handful is often scattered across different platforms, with no single view of all of it.


[[cracks]]
== Where things fell through the cracks

Before this workflow, it felt like pure chaos, _and_ I have the scars to prove it.
Fedora runs on mailing lists, Fedora Discussion topics, Matrix chat rooms, and Fedora Forge issues/tickets.
Furthermore, all of these are spread across dozens of teams, Working Groups (WGs), and Special Interest Groups (SIGs).
In short, there are a lot of different places to follow a lot of different things.

For a long stretch, the link:https://docs.fedoraproject.org/en-US/marketing/[Fedora Marketing Team] had a backlog of tickets.
In a few cases, contributors asked for talks and presentations to be uploaded to the Fedora YouTube channel.
I had the access to do it and other YouTube maintainers were no longer active, so therefore, it was quietly mine to own.
I saw the tickets at least once, but I was overworked and busy with higher-priority work.
The YouTube videos slid off my list.
A new volunteer eventually picked them up months or even a year later.

Additionally, Fedora also runs meetings well… on paper.
Fedora holds virtual text meetings in Matrix chat rooms with a bot that logs everything.
Each meeting has a clear start, a clear end, and annotated topics.
But a faithful log is not the same as follow-through.
For example, in AI/ML SIG meetings, we discuss a decision or someone takes an action item, and it gets logged.
But it does not always make it back into the relevant ticket or discussion post.
The record exists, but it does not connect itself to the places that need updating.

Patterns like this repeat themselves.
The examples above are merely specific examples of where things fall through the cracks.
However, there are things like this that happen everyday in different corners of the community too.


[[workflow]]
== The AI multi-repo workflow

So, here is the workflow I use now.
It does not get rid of all the chaos, but it helps me manage it much better.
First, I point an link:/tags/llm-gen-ai/[LLM-gen-AI] agent at a directory holding several cloned Git repositories.
For example, the link:https://forge.fedoraproject.org/ai-ml/docs[AI/ML SIG docs repository], link:https://forge.fedoraproject.org/ai-ml/tickets[AI/ML SIG tickets repository], and link:https://forge.fedoraproject.org/ai-ml/skills-library[Agent Skills library].
Then I ask it to analyze across all of them.

I also feed it meeting logs or summaries.
I ask it to cross-check them against open tickets and Fedora Discussion topics:

* Did we document the decisions?
* Did we track the action items?
* Is anyone still waiting on a reply?

Critically, I do not want it reading only files from my local `git` clones.
I want it to query the remotes too: the Fedora Forge API, issues, discussions, and more.
The underlying link:https://forgejo.org/docs/latest/user/api/usage/[Forgejo API] is enormous.
It lets the agent track the actual cadence a team works at, not just a snapshot.

[[trust]]
=== A word on trust and hallucination

I have to be honest here: the agent sometimes gets things wrong.
It will occasionally misread a thread or hallucinate a detail.
However, I do not think that disqualifies it.
Humans get things wrong too.
I would rather have slightly imperfect visibility into tens, hundreds, or even thousands of repositories than none at all.
Ultimately, the question is not whether the agent is ever wrong.
*The question is what level of risk you are willing to accept for the visibility you gain*.


[[example]]
== A real example: Reviewer guidelines across three repos

Our link:https://forge.fedoraproject.org/ai-ml/skills-library[Fedora Agent Skills Library] needed reviewer guidelines.
Laura Barcziová (link:https://forge.fedoraproject.org/lbarczio[@lbarczio]), on our reviewer team, wrote a solid `REVIEWING.md` in the `ai-ml/skills-library` repo.
But our separate `ai-ml/docs` repository only had a page on creating a skill, and nothing on reviewing one.

I used an agent in link:https://opencode.ai/[OpenCode] to pull discussion context from Fedora Forge issues about our reviewer process.
It helped me reconcile that context with Laura's draft.
Then I orchestrated multiple Pull Requests across the repositories at once.
This brought the AI/ML SIG docs site in line with what the team had actually decided.


[[payoff]]
== The real payoff is not the tool

In conclusion, the payoff is that it hands me back the capacity to zoom out.
In a project where nobody can hold it all in their head, something should watch dozens of repositories.
It surfaces what changed and what needs to catch up.
*That means I can engage with people's feedback instead of just surviving the volume of it*.
When someone takes the time to review my Pull Request, I want the bandwidth to genuinely consider what they said.
I do not want to just clear my queue.

I hope this overview of my flow and process is insightful.
